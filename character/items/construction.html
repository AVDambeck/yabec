Construction

Permission

Players need permission from local rulers to undertake construction. For siege weapons and water vessels, this may be a simple request of fee for a permit. For buildings, this may involve the complex process of acquiring land rights.

When constructing in the wilderness, players instead face logistical challenges.

Clear Land

If building in the wilderness, all monsters in a 6 mile area must be killed or driven off.

Design

The player creates a plan for what they would like to build, and calculates the construction cost. Then seeks approval from the dungeon master.

Costs and Time

Construction requires 1 day per 5000 cp, and one engineer per 1,000,000 cp (whose upkeep must be paid each week), and .

Hiring dwarves quarters the construction time, but requires the approval of a dwarven chieftain, and increases the cost 10 fold.

Repairing a heavily damaged construction costs half as much and takes a tenth as long. Repairing a lightly damaged construction costs a quarter as much and takes a twentieth as long

Having a Stronghold

Costs of Ownership

Maintaining the physical buildings and infrastructure costs 1-5% of the total price of the stronghold per year. This accounts for normal wear and tear, damage due to weathering or misfortune or the like. Failing to pay for this causes the stronghold to fall into disrepair and eventually into ruin.

Any servants, mercenaries, specialists and the like must be paid their agreed rates on a weekly. If the servants, specialists or mercenaries require special facilities, materials or the like, these must be provided regularly and in good repair

Livestock, horses and the like must be properly sheltered, fed and watered.

Income from Strongholds

Strongholds do not typically have a source of income. Stronghold owners that attempt such tolls without a clear mandate from some remote ruler (and even if such a mandate exists) are seen as little more than extortionists and "bandit lords", forcing fees upon travelers on threat of punishment by the Stronghold's owner. However, this does not deter some owners from "collecting their fair share for bringing peace to an area"
