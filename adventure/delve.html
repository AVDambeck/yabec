Dungeon Exploration

This is the main game. It's dungeon time, fuck yeahh.

Sequence of Play per Turn

A turn represents roughly 10 minutes of game time, or one activity in the dungeon.

The DM will knock their table to mark the end of a dungeon turn.

  1. Activity: The party decides what to do, e.g. moving, searching, listening, investigating, talking, fighting.
  2. Description: The dungeon master describes what happens, what is seen, if monsters are encountered, etc.
  3. Hazards: The dungeon master checks hazards.

Hazards

A hazard check encompases a variety of time based treats to adventurers. The dungeon master rolls a d6 to determine the hazard.

d6 Effect Description
1 Nothing The adventure continues.
2 Burn Torches burn out, and lanterns need fresh oil.
3 Fatigue Lose well-rested or become fatigued. If already fatigued, take 1d4 damage.
4 Shift Traps reset, doors swing close, near-death players die, etc.
5-6 Encounter Chance of random encounter with a monster.

Movement

Marching Order

While moving through narrow doors and halls, movement is restricted. The marching order is decided by the players. In general, only the front and the back are significant.

Travel Distance

If nothing else ends the turn, moving through three zones (i.e. three rooms/hallways) is a turn. By moving quickly through a familiar area, you can move up to nine zones instead.

Doors

Eavesdropping at a door or corridor can reveal what lies beyond. Roll a d6, and add the bonus from your wisdom score. Some classes, like thieves, have additional bonuses.

On a result of six or more, the dungeon master will reveal sounds you are able to hear.

When opening a door, check your strength. If you fail, the door makes a loud sound. Otherwise, the door opens silently. Only a player at the front of the marching order can open doors; opening a door implies moving to the front. If there is not a door, a player can instead check their dexterity to approach silently.

Physically destroying a door takes a full turn, requires appropriate tools, and makes loud noise.

Encounters

Surprise

Players can surprise monsters when opening a door quietly, or charge into a room. Players are unable to surprise monsters when carrying a light in a dark area, and after making a loud noise. Monsters can surprise players when hidden in a room, or after stalking the players for one turn.

Surprised creatures take no actions the first round of combat, are automatically hit by attacks, and make a morale check.

Reaction

The dungeon master might roll to determine monster reaction, especially on random encounters. The dungeon master might include a reaction modifier from either the players in the front or a player initiating a confrontation.

2d6 Result
<=2 Attacks
3–5 Hostile, may attack
6–8 Uncertain, confused
9–11 Indifferent, may negotiate
12 or more Eager, friendly

Wandering Monster

The traditional method calls for a 1-in-6 chance every other turn. To match theses odds exactly with the hazard die, roll a d4 for each check, with a result of a 1 indicating a monster. I like to use a d6 with the following results: a 1 is always a monster. 2 is a monster or an omen depending on if it is a dangerous area, if the players have been especially noisy, etc. 3 is always an omen. 4 through 6 are safe.

Monsters appear 1d4 zones away.

Evade

When two groups encounter one another, one or both may decide to evade the other, or one group may decide to pursue the evading group.

  • If both choose to evade, nothing happens.
  • If one chooses to evade, and is not pursued, nothing happens.
  • If one chooses to evade, and is pursued, the chance of successfully evading is 3-in-6. If they fail, begin combat.
  • If a group is surprised, they are unable to evade nor pursue.

Mapping

Once the encounter (if any) is settled, players are able to observe the room for long enough to add to their map.

Misc

Searching

Very little treasure will be obvious at first glance. Searching:

  • Reveals all non-obvious features, such as traps.
  • Will always reveal everything it can.
  • Takes a minimum of three people per zone.
  • Is a turn.

Rest

Ignore the fatigue hazard while resting. A wandering monster will disrupt a rest.

Taking a turn to rest removes fatigue. If not fatigued, end spells that last until fatigue and become well-rested.

Light

There are three levels of light:

  • Dark: The darkness is pitch black, and prevents sight. Imposes blindness.
  • Dim: Allows some detail to be seen.
  • Bright: Allows full vision, including details and reading. Disables infravision.

A light source casts bright light within touch, and dim within close.

Number of sources

To illuminate a whole party, one torch is needed for every five adventurers (i.e. PCs, hirelings, and other companions). If there are not enough torches, some members will be in darkness.

Infravision

A special type of vision had by many monsters, and some players: seeing heat energy that radiates of living things. Negate the effect of dark. When a creature with Infravision suddenly goes from dark to bright, they are blinded for one round, and their infravision is disabled until they spend a full turn in the dark.

It is not possible to read in the dim or dark with infravision.

Zones

The dungeon environment is split into zones. As a rule of thumb, each room and hallway is a zone. Large rooms or hallways may be multipole zones.

Guidelines

  • Architecture: Architectural features, such as doorways, should divide zones.
  • 40': Rooms bigger than 40', and hallways longer than 40', should be multiple zones.
  • Even: Large rooms and hallways should have their zones divided evenly.
  • Whole Number: Large rooms and hallways should always have a whole number of zones.

Ranges

Ranges and relative location are divided into four ranges:

  • Touch: Striking distance.
  • Close: Throwing distance. Speaking distance. With in the same or adjacent zone.
  • Distant: Shouting distance. Upto 2 zones away. Farthest distance to make out details (in bright light).
  • Sight: Anything that can be seen. Up to 3 miles in ideal conditions.

Lost in the Dungeon

Should the session end with the adventurers unsure of a way out, the dungon master can employ the following table. Each player rolls a d12, adds their level, and subtracts the level of the dungeon they are on.

d12 Result
<=2 You are consumed by the horrors below. You die and your body is unrecoverable.
3 You are slain and rise from the dead as a wight. It carries your equipment, and slaying it will be required to ressurect you. The dungeon master adds it to the wandering monsters.
4-5 You die somewhere in the dungeon.
6 You are possesed! The dungeon master adds you to the wandering monsters. When encountered, you will be hostile, but can be captured and exorcized.
7 You are lost and starving in the dungeon. Roll again at the start of next session.
8 You are captured! You party members can pay randsom of 50gp x your level, or leave you hostage in the dungeon.
9-10 You suffer a terrible injury. Roll dismemberment.
11-12 You have survived!
>=13 You have survived, and the gods look favorably on you! If lawful: learn the location of a treasure. If chaotic: learn something about an enemy. If neutral: gain 10 xp.
