Introduciton

This section describes the very basics of how to play. The following sections will describe game procedures for a variaty of situations.

Roleplay

In Yabec, you play as one adventurer in a fantasy world. You may have been given a pregenorated character, or have made one yourself. This is your avatar, and the way you interact with the game world.

The situation conversation

The Dungeon Master (DM) is a player with a special role. They are a referee, narrator, and show runner, and play the game world, instead of a character.

The dungeon master will describe the situation in the game world, as it is observable to your character. You might ask clerifying questions about the situation. But eventually the dungeon master will ask you a question. When you make your choice, the dungeon master will resolve your choice, possibly using dice, or another game procedure.

Either way, the dungeon master will describe the results of your action, and advance the situation.

Teamwork

The group acts togeather. Sometimes characters do act individually. However the group should act in a coordinated manner. The dungeon master should not allow one character to act against the interests of the group. The dungeon master will often wait to resolve one character's actions until the actions of the group are settled.

Overview of an adventure

Most adventures begin in a town. There players can, often independently, talk to the inhabitants, buy equipment, and search for rumors. Then they, as a group, delve into a dungeon. In the dungeon they map its hallways, search its rooms, and try to steal its treasure without being killed by its monsters. Then they return to town and divide up the treasure and EXP.

Most sessions, they will get involved in at least one combat. from time to time they may need to negotate. Once and a while, they may travel to a different area of the world.

The Three Games

This basic game is exclusivly a dungeon crawl. Players go room by room, clearing floor after floor.

The advanced game expands the perview of the advenure. Players also explore towns and complete multiple quest lines, perhaps a negotation with a local ruler, or a journey to multiple adventuring locations.

The expert game makes players powerful rulers, and parts of the game world itself. Players build strongholds, raise armies, and negotate with kings.

Turns

The most important resource is time. Turns, sometimes called "dungeon turns", are intervals about 10 mintues of game time. Each turn, many things happen in the background: torches burn slowly, monsters lurk in the darkness, guards go on patrol. The dungeon master will knock twice on the table to subtly convey the end/begining of a turn, and will announce events that come with it.

Rounds and Initiative

Within a turn, when there is a great time pressure, such as during a chace or during combat, the dungeon master will announce initiative.

At the start of each round, the dungeon master and the players each roll a d6. If the players roll higher or equal to the DM, they go first. Otherwise, the monsters go first.

Each round, each character can take only one action. Then they must wait for the next round to act again.

Optional: Individual Initiative / Willed Actions

Instead of annoucing initiative, the dungeon master will announce willing of actions at the start of each round. Players discuss, and then announce the type of action they wish to take. The initiative die they roll is on the table below.

Players who roll a four or higher act before monsters. Players who roll three or lower act after.

Checks

When you dungeon master asks you for a check, roll the appropriate dice, and try to roll under, or equal to, the target number. Tell the dungeon master if you succeed, or fail.

The die is usually a d20, and the target number depends on the type of check.

The most common type of check is an ability check, but you may also have to roll under your physical or magical save values.

To check, roll a d20. You succeed if the d20 <= your ability score/save value.

Attacking

Attack checks (also often called attack rolls) have a have a hidden target number called an armor class, or AC. If the dungeon master has not announced the AC of a monster, simply tell the DM the value of your check.

To check your attack, subtract a d20 from your AV (attack value). If it is less than or equal to the enemy's AC, you succeed in your hit and must roll damage.

Dodging

Dodge checks (oftern called a dodge rolls), have a hidden target number called an attack value, or AV. If the dungeon mater has not announced the AV of a monster, simply tell the dungeon master the value of your check.

To check your dodge, roll a d20 and add your AC. If it is less than or equal to the AV, you succeed.

Advantage

When making a check with advantage, attempt twice. If you succeed on either roll, you succeed the check.

Disadvantage

When making a roll with disadvantage, attempt twice. If you fail on either roll, you fail the check.

Cancelation and Stacking

One disadvantage and one advantage cancel out.

You cannot have multiple advantage stacks or disadvantage stacks.
